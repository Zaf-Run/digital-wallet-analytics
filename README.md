# Digital Wallet Analytics

Customer Engagement, Transaction Performance, Wallet Balances, and Revenue Analytics

Dataset: PaySim mobile money simulation (6,362,620 transactions, 31 simulated days).
Stack: SQL (DuckDB), Python (pandas, matplotlib), Power BI.
Runs on a laptop with 4 GB RAM. No server.

## 1. Executive summary

A mobile money operator needs to know who is active, what they do, how often payments fail, how much money sits in wallets, and what earns revenue. This project answers those questions with SQL on a 6.36 million row transaction log, then checks every headline number with an independent pandas calculation.

The most useful result is a clear boundary. PaySim supports transaction mix, value, cancellation, fraud pattern and balance analysis well. It does not support customer retention, registration cohorts, fee revenue, failure reasons or channel analysis. Those gaps are documented, and where a proxy or an assumption is used, it is labelled in the table, the SQL and the dashboard.

### Recommendation

Primary dataset: PaySim. Reasons: it is the only candidate I have verified, it has the scale to make DAU, value and balance work meaningful, it has real operational field structure (types, balances before and after), and its provenance is documented by the authors.

Supplementary options, not used in this version:
- Berka, to repeat the balance analysis on real anonymised bank data.
- The Kaggle wallet dataset, to demonstrate transaction status analysis. Treat it as a separate small study. It cannot be joined to PaySim because there is no shared key.

### What PaySim can and cannot measure

| Required metric | Status | How handled |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction count, value, average amount | Supported | Direct |
| Successful vs failed | Partly | Cancelled fraud is the only non success state. Labelled `CANCELLED_FRAUD`. It is not a technical or insufficient funds failure. |
| Failure reasons | Not supported | Not invented. Type and fraud pattern shown instead. |
| Channel | Not supported | `channel_proxy` from type: Agent (cash in, cash out), Merchant (payment), P2P (transfer). Labelled proxy. |
| DAU, MAU | Supported, with a warning | Initiating account with at least one completed transaction. |
| New, returning, inactive | Partly | "New" means first appearance in the log, not registration. Inactive is not measurable without a prior base. |
| Wallet balance snapshots | Not supported | Last observed post transaction balance per customer account. Not an end of day snapshot. |
| Fees, revenue | Not supported | Modelled from an assumed tariff in `fee_assumptions`. Not observed. |
| Refunds, chargebacks, partner fees, net revenue | Not supported | Not calculated. |
| Retries | No retry identifier | Each row is treated as one unique attempt. |

## 3. Project charter (Phase 2)

Business problem. Product and operations teams at a mobile money or wallet provider need a single, trustworthy view of usage, payment performance, float held in wallets and earnings. Without it, they cannot judge whether growth is healthy or where leakage occurs.

Business objectives
1. Measure active usage and its stability.
2. Quantify transaction volume and value by type and by day.
3. Measure the cancellation rate and understand where it concentrates.
4. Describe how much money customers hold in wallets.
5. Estimate which transaction types and ticket sizes drive revenue, under stated assumptions.

Research questions
1. How many unique customers transact per day and per month?
2. How is activity distributed over the month?
3. How often does a customer transact?
4. Which transaction types carry the most volume and value?
5. Where do cancellations concentrate, and what pattern identifies them?
6. What is the distribution of customer balances?
7. Which types and amount bands drive modelled revenue?

Stakeholders: Head of Digital Wallet product, Payments operations, Risk and fraud team, Finance (revenue assurance), Marketing and CRM.

Scope. In: one month of ledger data, customer (C prefix) and merchant (M prefix) accounts. Out: pricing optimisation, forecasting, machine learning.

Terminology used throughout
- Transaction volume: count of transactions.
- Transaction value: sum of amounts. Not revenue.
- Revenue: fees earned by the provider. Not observed here.
- Balance: money held in a wallet at a point in time. Not a flow.
- Payment failure: a payment attempt that does not complete (declined, timed out, insufficient funds). Not observed here.
- Cancelled fraud: transaction stopped because it was identified as fraud. This is the only non completed state in PaySim.
- Refund: money returned to the payer by the merchant. Not observed.
- Chargeback: forced reversal initiated through the card scheme or issuer. Not observed.

Link to real operations. Cash in and cash out run through agent networks, so they drive float and liquidity management. Payments to merchants earn a merchant discount. Transfers earn customer fees and carry the highest fraud exposure. The same KPIs (active users, success rate, value by type, balance float, take rate) appear in the daily operating reports of mobile money providers and digital banks.

## 4. Method

1. `sql/00_load.sql` loads the CSV into DuckDB and adds `txn_id`, `day_no`, `hour_of_day`, `status_proxy`, `channel_proxy`.
2. `sql/01` to `sql/11` calculate each KPI. Each file has a header comment with its definition.
3. `run_analysis.py` runs all SQL and writes CSVs to `outputs/`.
4. `validate.py` recomputes the key figures in pandas and runs data quality tests.
5. Power BI loads the CSVs in `outputs/` (see `docs/powerbi_spec.md`).

Definitions
- Active user (DAU, MAU): distinct initiating account (`nameOrig`) with at least one completed transaction in the period. Alternative reported: any customer account (C prefix) as sender or recipient.
- Successful: `is_fraud = 0`.
- Closing balance: balance after the account's last completed event in the month, as sender or recipient, customer accounts only.
- Modelled revenue: completed value multiplied by the assumed rate in `fee_assumptions.csv`.

## 5. Findings

### Transaction mix

| Type | Share of count | Share of attempted value | Cancellation rate | Average amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CASH_OUT | 35.2% | 34.5% | 0.184% | 176,274 |
| PAYMENT | 33.8% | 2.5% | 0% | 13,058 |
| CASH_IN | 22.0% | 20.7% | 0% | 168,920 |
| TRANSFER | 8.4% | 42.4% | 0.769% | 910,647 |
| DEBIT | 0.7% | 0.02% | 0% | 5,484 |

Transfers are 8% of transactions but 42% of value. Payments are 34% of transactions but 2.5% of value. Count and value tell different stories, so both must be on the dashboard.

### Engagement

- Activity is uneven. Days 1, 2 and 6 to 17 carry about 350,000 to 574,000 completed transactions a day. Days 3 to 5 and 18 to 30 carry between about 1,000 and 58,000 attempts a day. Day 3 has only 760 completed transactions.
- Day 13 completed value is 138.2 billion against 57.6 billion on day 12, with similar counts. A small number of very large tickets drive value spikes.
- 99.85% of initiating accounts (6,335,852 of 6,345,122) transact once. Only 9,255 transact twice and 15 three times.
- Receiving customer accounts behave differently. 146,418 receive ten or more completed transactions. These look like agent or aggregator accounts, not ordinary customers, but the data does not label them.
- Using the any role definition, the returning share of daily active accounts rises from 9% on day 2 to about 26% on day 13 and 33% on day 29. This reflects accounts reappearing in the log, not retention.

### Cancellations (fraud)

- 8,213 cancelled transactions, all TRANSFER (4,097) or CASH_OUT (4,116). Value 12.06 billion.
- Fraud median amount is 441,423 against 171,034 for completed transfers and cash outs.
- 97.6% of cancelled transactions (8,018) empty the sender's balance exactly (amount equals opening balance). Zero completed transactions in the data match that pattern.
- The legacy rule flag caught 16 of 8,213 (0.19%). All 16 were true fraud, so precision is perfect but recall is almost nil.
- A simple "transfer above 200,000" rule would hit 1,192,198 completed transfers and cash outs for every 2,740 fraudulent transfers. Amount alone is a poor control.
- Cancellation count is near constant at 216 to 320 per day regardless of volume. As a result the daily cancellation rate swings from 0.05% on busy days to 29% on day 3, and day 31 has 272 attempts, all cancelled.

### Wallet balances

- 6,912,642 customer accounts have a last observed balance. Mean 905,775. Median 0. 75th percentile 202,865. 90th percentile 2.28 million. 99th percentile 16.1 million.
- 53.2% of accounts end at zero. Of accounts last seen as senders, 56.7% end at zero. Of accounts last seen as recipients, 14.4%.
- Accounts with 1 million or more hold 92.8% of observed balance (915,896 accounts). Float is highly concentrated.

### Revenue (modelled, assumption driven)

Rates assumed: cash in 0%, cash out 1.0%, transfer 0.5%, merchant payment 0.8%, debit 0%.

| Type | Modelled revenue | Share |
|---|---|---|
| CASH_OUT | 3.88 billion | 59.7% |
| TRANSFER | 2.40 billion | 36.8% |
| PAYMENT | 0.22 billion | 3.5% |
| CASH_IN, DEBIT | 0 | 0% |

- Implied take rate on completed value: 0.57%.
- Fees not earned on cancelled transactions: about 90 million, or 1.4% of modelled revenue.
- By ticket size, 100k to 1M tickets earn 65.4% of modelled revenue and tickets of 1M and above earn 24.9% from only 2.0% of transactions. Tickets under 10k earn 0.7%.
- Because these are assumed rates, treat the ranking by type and by band as sensitive to the tariff. Change `fee_assumptions` and rerun to test scenarios.

## 6. Validation and data quality

Cross check. Totals for completed count, completed value, cancelled value, MAU, average DAU, modelled revenue, account count, median balance and zero balance share match between SQL and pandas. Mean closing balance differs by 4 units on 905,775 (0.0004%) because of a different tie break for events in the same hour. This is documented, not hidden.

Data quality tests in `validate.py`:
- No duplicate rows. No negative values. 16 zero amount rows.
- Origin balance arithmetic holds for 100% of CASH_IN but only 11.5% of CASH_OUT, 4.8% of TRANSFER, 48.8% of PAYMENT and 71.6% of DEBIT. Balance columns are not a reliable bookkeeping ledger in PaySim.
- 56.6% of transactions have an amount above the sender's opening balance. In a real wallet these would be declined for insufficient funds. Here they completed.
- Merchant (M) accounts always show a zero balance. They are not tracked.
- The PaySim authors state that balances on cancelled fraud rows should not be used for fraud modelling. This project excludes those rows from balance analysis.

Why DAU/MAU is not meaningful here. Almost every account initiates one transaction in the month, so MAU approaches total transaction count and DAU/MAU falls to 3.34%. In a real wallet that would signal very poor stickiness. Here it reflects how the simulator assigns account identifiers. Report it with this caveat, or use it as a worked example of why metric definitions must match data design.

## 7. Recommendations

For the business (conditional on the limits above)
1. Add a balance drain rule on transfers and cash outs: sender amount equals opening balance, especially for first time or very new accounts. In this data it catches 97.6% of cancelled fraud with no false positives. Real customers do withdraw full balances, so test it in monitor mode first and combine with recipient history and velocity.
2. Replace or retune the legacy amount rule. It caught 0.19% of fraud.
3. Report cancellation rate as a rate and a count. A fixed number of cancellations makes the daily rate unstable on low volume days.
4. Track float concentration. Accounts holding 1M or more are 13% of customer accounts and hold 92.8% of observed balance. Liquidity planning and agent rebalancing should focus on that group.
5. Protect revenue from large tickets. Tickets above 100k generate over 90% of modelled revenue, so service outages or fraud controls that hit those tickets matter most.
6. Capture the fields this project could not measure: customer registration date, transaction status with reason code, retry identifier, channel, fee and partner cost, refund and chargeback records. Without them DAU/MAU, funnel and net revenue cannot be trusted.

For the project (next steps)
- Repeat the balance study on Berka.
- Add a small status analysis using the Kaggle wallet dataset after verifying its license and structure.

## 8. Limitations

- PaySim is synthetic. Findings describe the simulator, not a real operator.
- No calendar date. Weekday effects cannot be tested. "Day" is a simulated day.
- No customer registration, so cohorts and churn cannot be measured.
- Fee revenue is modelled from assumed rates.
- Cancelled means fraud cancelled. It is not a technical payment failure.
- Currency is not stated. Values are in simulated currency units.
